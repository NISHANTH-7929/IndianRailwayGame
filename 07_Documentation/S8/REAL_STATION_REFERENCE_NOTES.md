## Observations and production consequences

- Images 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 show station scale, repeated long roof runs, covered
  footbridges, multiple track corridors and planted boundaries. They do not
  supply surveyed track spacing or enough detail to invent a working yard.
- Image 2 shows an older station with blue railings, cream/red walls, sheet
  roof discoloration, benches and trackside clutter. Roof colors differ from
  the newer bright blue installations in other images.
- Image 5 is visibly labelled Renigunta: pink ornamented facade, blue glazing,
  curved blue awnings, red coping wall, repeated yellow platform marks and
  service pipes alongside the track. This should not be mixed indiscriminately
  with the other stations' landmark architecture.
- Image 8 is the strongest underside/paving/light reference: parallel tile
  courses, yellow edge strip, pale steel roof framing, tubes, suspended boards
  and uneven surface reflections. Its night lighting is not a daylight color
  measurement, and its coach is not a reference for replacing the LHB identity.
- Image 9 is visibly labelled Babina: a shallow gable of blue corrugated sheets,
  layered purlins and trusses, pale columns on dark plinths, wide yellow fascia
  signs, red platform wall with worn white coping, grey paving, dusty irregular
  ballast, rust-brown rail sides, shiny rail heads and complex OHE fittings.
  Tree masses form a backdrop rather than a bare ground horizon.

The platform-level references (8 and 9) should drive playable-distance detail.
The aerial references guide composition only until a specific station/layout is
selected. Current S7 platform length/berth remain authoritative; a true full-size
station requires its own separately validated route configuration.
